# Project Brief: Community Sports Platform for Azerbaijan

**Status:** Framing brief | **Date:** 2026-03-21

---

## The Idea

The long-term idea is a community sports platform for Azerbaijan: one product that helps people organize games, discover venues, and participate across multiple sports.

The repo no longer treats that broad vision as the immediate product scope. At this stage, it is a directional platform thesis, not a finalized roadmap.

## Current Wedge

The most credible entry point is narrower:

- start in **Baku**
- start with **soccer as the first local wedge**
- focus first on **organizer reliability and game coordination**

That means the early product question is not "How do we build everything for every sport?" It is "Can we reduce the weekly coordination burden enough that organizers and players change behavior?"

## The Problem

Organizing recreational sports in Baku is still too manual. Organizers rely on WhatsApp or Telegram threads, phone calls, and ad hoc payment collection. Players struggle to judge whether an unfamiliar game is real, suitable, and worth committing to. Venue information is fragmented and often hard to verify.

The result is familiar:

- too much organizer effort
- too many last-minute dropouts and replacements
- unclear venue choices and booking friction
- weak trust when players are outside the usual friend circle

There are strong analog products globally, but no clear local category leader for this exact workflow in Azerbaijan.

## Who It's For

- **Organizers** who repeatedly coordinate games, collect money, and manage attendance
- **Players** who want a more reliable path into local games, including outside their existing group
- **Venue operators** who want more credible demand without adding heavy admin work

## Long-Term Platform Potential

If the wedge works, the broader platform could eventually include:

- cross-sport game creation and discovery
- venue discovery and booking
- recurring game management
- payment and cancellation handling
- lightweight trust, identity, and reliability layers
- later-stage retention features such as history, ratings, or progression where justified

These are platform possibilities, not current commitments.

## Launch Logic

Soccer is the practical starting point because it is the strongest local participation wedge and Baku gives the highest chance of early density. If the first loop works there, the underlying coordination logic can later extend to other sports.

## The Opportunity

- Azerbaijan is digitally ready, with high internet and electronic-payment adoption
- Baku provides the population density needed for repeated local coordination
- The problem already exists without a strong local software layer solving it cleanly
- Global analogs show that community sports coordination, organizer tooling, and venue workflows can support real businesses

## Current Stage

This repository is still in discovery. It is not at full PRD stage and it is not ready for detailed implementation planning.

The current objective is to validate:

- whether the organizer reliability wedge is strong enough
- which trust cues matter most for unfamiliar games
- whether payment and cancellation rules are locally acceptable
- whether Baku supply density is strong enough to support a pilot

## Vision

Long term, the product can become the default way people organize and play recreational sports across Azerbaijan. Short term, it has to earn that ambition by proving a narrower wedge first.
